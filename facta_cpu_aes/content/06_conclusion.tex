\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
This study quantified how much of an AES implementation advantage survives a broader measurement boundary. The Oracle Arm64 results establish strong sensitivity to implementation and capability selection, with default OpenSSL reaching approximately nineteen times the transform throughput of the evaluated alternatives. The central Windows comparison shows that OpenSSL's relative advantage decreases from approximately $1.93\times$ for transformation to $1.23\times$ for the instrumented operation. Broader-operation throughput retains 26.7\% of the OpenSSL transform rate and 41.8\% of the PyCryptodome rate, without identifying the costs of individual overhead components.

The contribution is a reproducible characterization of the limits of translating cipher-processing performance into software-operation performance. Supporting CBC and GCM observations reinforce the need to preserve message size and cryptographic functionality when interpreting throughput. Implementation selection should therefore be evaluated together with the operation that invokes it. Matched interfaces, instruction-level profiling and complete request benchmarks are needed to establish causes and application benefits beyond these measured configurations.
