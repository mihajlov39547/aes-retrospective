This paper evaluates GPU computing for accelerating bulk AES encryption in
telecommunications-oriented processing, where higher throughput must offset the cost of moving
data through an accelerator. Validated AES-128 CTR and ECB implementations are compared with
hardware-assisted CPU AES-NI across three heterogeneous systems, using paired workloads and five
sessions per system. Measurements distinguish device-resident execution from a prepared-buffer
pipeline including synchronous host--device transfers and synchronization. For 32~MiB CTR
buffers, median paired pipeline speedups reach $1.354\times$ on T600 and $4.761\times$ on Tesla
T4 relative to their respective CPU implementations, with gains in all five sessions. GTX
achieves $0.935\times$, showing that the benefit depends on the configuration. These results
demonstrate that GPU-assisted AES-CTR can outperform hardware-accelerated CPU encryption under
the tested transfer-inclusive conditions, providing an experimental basis for bulk cryptographic
processing in heterogeneous telecommunications architectures.
