\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

Figure~\ref{fig:aesni-scopes} presents median paired GPU speedups across the five input sizes,
separately for AES-128 ECB and CTR and for resident and prepared-buffer execution. At 32~MiB,
resident execution exceeds the corresponding AES-NI reference on all three systems, in both
modes and in every session. Pipeline results differ by platform and mode: CTR gains occur in all
five sessions on T600 and T4, whereas the remaining platform--mode combinations have mixed or
consistently slower pipeline results.

For T600 CTR, the median pipeline speedup of $1.354\times$ corresponds to approximately 26.14\%
lower latency, calculated from the median paired CPU-to-GPU timing ratio. Its session range
remains above parity, from $1.129\times$ to $1.391\times$. The paired speedup has an
interquartile range of 0.030896 and a sample coefficient of variation of approximately 8.18\%.
These statistics describe the five session-level ratios used for this configuration.

T4 CTR has the largest median pipeline speedup at 32~MiB, $4.761\times$, with a five-session
range of $4.691$--$5.021\times$. For both T600 and T4 CTR, every recorded session favors the
pipeline even though the measured interval includes transfers and synchronization. The resident
speedups are larger than the pipeline speedups on both systems, as they are for every
platform--mode combination in Table~\ref{tab:gpu-speedups}.

GTX CTR has a pipeline median of $0.935\times$ and favors the GPU in one session. Its range,
$0.918$--$1.008\times$, crosses parity. GTX ECB remains below parity in all five sessions. T600
ECB has a median above one, $1.118\times$, but only three sessions favor the GPU, with a range
of $0.659$--$1.144\times$. T4 ECB remains slower than AES-NI throughout the five sessions
despite resident speedups above one. Thus, the positive T600 ECB median coexists with mixed
session outcomes, while the T600 and T4 CTR medians accompany exclusively positive outcomes.

Table~\ref{tab:gpu-throughput} reports absolute GPU throughput at the same input size. T4 has
the highest resident and pipeline CTR rates among the tested systems. GTX resident CTR
throughput exceeds T600 resident throughput, while the ordering is reversed for the pipeline. On
each system, the pipeline rate is below the corresponding resident rate in both encryption
modes.

The T4 GPU rates are nearly identical across modes: approximately 3713~MB/s for ECB and
3714~MB/s for CTR in the pipeline, and 9177 and 9167~MB/s, respectively, in resident execution.
In contrast, its host-relative pipeline speedups are $0.893\times$ for ECB and $4.761\times$ for
CTR. The host AES-NI ECB/CTR throughput ratio has a session median of $5.378\times$ and a range
of $5.250$--$5.795\times$. The corresponding host ratios on GTX and T600 are approximately
$1.26\times$. The T4 host therefore records higher ECB than CTR throughput in every session,
while its measured GPU throughput changes little between modes.

The smaller workloads in Figure~\ref{fig:aesni-scopes} show different acceleration outcomes. At
1~KiB, both resident and pipeline paths are slower than AES-NI in every session, on all systems
and in both modes. At 16~KiB, all pipeline configurations remain slower in every session.
Resident T4 CTR has a median above parity at that size, with gains in four sessions; the other
resident configurations remain below parity throughout. At 1, 16, and 32~MiB, resident execution
is faster than AES-NI in all sessions for every platform and mode.

Table~\ref{tab:ctr-size-results} summarizes CTR pipeline results across the size grid. T600
gains occur in all sessions at 1 and 32~MiB, but in only one session at 16~MiB. T4 gains occur
in all sessions at each of the three MiB-scale inputs. GTX has four favorable sessions at 1~MiB,
none at 16~MiB, and one at 32~MiB; no tested size has five favorable GTX pipeline sessions. ECB
pipeline results likewise contain no five-session improvement on GTX or T4. T600 ECB favors the
GPU in four, two, and three sessions at 1, 16, and 32~MiB, respectively.

Absolute throughput is non-monotonic across the size grid. On GTX, throughput at 16~MiB is below
both the 1~MiB and 32~MiB rates in all five sessions, for both modes and both GPU timing
boundaries. On T4, this local reduction appears in resident throughput in four sessions for each
mode, while neither pipeline mode shows the same pattern. The session counts therefore differ
between platforms and between the two timing boundaries on T4.



\subsection{Oracle Cloud ARM64: CPU Implementation and Envelope Controls}
\label{sec:oracle-results}
On the independent Oracle Cloud ARM64 host, the five-session 32~MiB
AES-128-CTR study produced approximately 4.03~GB/s of native
OpenSSL-default transform-only throughput, compared with approximately
209~MB/s for the PyCryptodome path and 218~MB/s for the
\texttt{OPENSSL\_armcap=0} control. These values describe the specific
library implementations and timed boundaries; they are not GPU speedups.
The default OpenSSL path was approximately 19.3 times faster than the
recorded PyCryptodome transform path at this size, with the important
qualification that Python/FFI and C-native interval definitions differ.

In a separate host-side envelope experiment, a single worker achieved
approximately 1.06~GB/s at 32~MiB, while two concurrent workers reached
approximately 2.03~GB/s in aggregate, or about 1.92 times the
single-worker aggregate rate. These campaigns are not paired
per-repetition observations and their envelope boundary differs from the
4.03~GB/s native transform interval. The contrast demonstrates that
buffer lifecycle and concurrency must be considered before extending
cryptographic core throughput to a complete service. No Oracle result is
used to calculate a speedup against the Tesla T4 or other GPU system.

\subsection{Revisiting the Host Baseline on the Tesla T4}
\label{sec:new-t4-baseline}
The historical $4.761\times$ T4 CTR speedup in the preceding tables is an
\emph{earlier PyCryptodome-relative result}, not a speedup relative to an
optimized native OpenSSL implementation. In a new five-session T4 campaign,
32~MiB AES-128-CTR yields a $5.586\times$ median session ratio against
PyCryptodome but only $0.942\times$ against native OpenSSL when the GPU
prepared pipeline is the denominator. The OpenSSL transform throughput is
approximately 3.874~GB/s and the GPU prepared-pipeline throughput is
approximately 3.646~GB/s. When only GPU resident computation is considered,
the native OpenSSL CPU/GPU ratio is $2.299\times$. Thus the improved CPU
baseline reverses the prepared-pipeline ranking, without implying that the
historical measurements were incorrect.

\subsection{Independent Pages: A Compute Advantage That Does Not Survive Transfers}
\label{sec:page-results}

to prepared CUDA pipeline time ratios in the page-batching study. Every
reported ratio is below unity across all five sessions. The gap narrows at
larger batch sizes but does not reverse direction.

The most informative case is a batch of 32 independent pages. The median CPU
native transform duration is approximately 8.362~ms, compared with 3.714~ms
for one GPU-resident kernel and 9.153~ms for the prepared GPU pipeline.
Table~\ref{tab:page-32-components} separates the measurements. The
compute-only ratio is $2.251\times$ in favor of the GPU, whereas the
transfer-inclusive ratio is $0.913\times$ and favors the CPU. Both directions
are consistent across all five sessions. The observed H2D-plus-D2H component
is about 5.418~ms. Medians of nested or separately timed components cannot
be added as if they described a single observation.

The page-aware GPU prepared pipeline shows almost no relative penalty against
a single continuous CTR stream of the same size: its page/continuous
throughput ratios are approximately $1.0007\times$ at 4~MiB,
$1.0068\times$ at 16~MiB and $1.0066\times$ at 32~MiB. These sub-percent
differences should not be described as a robust optimization gain. They
support the narrower observation that independent per-page counter handling
does not impose a large measured GPU pipeline overhead in the evaluated
implementation. This is distinct from demonstrating an advantage from
batching over sequential per-page GPU launches, which was not the control
experiment.
