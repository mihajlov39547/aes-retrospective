\subsection{Offloading Advantage and Timing Scope}

grid. The numerical contrast at 32~MiB is summarized in Table~\ref{tab:gpu-speedups}. Each
comparison uses the corresponding system's AES-NI reference and the estimators defined in
Section~\ref{subsec:statistical_analysis}.

Resident execution exceeds AES-NI in all five sessions for both modes on every system at
32~MiB. Transfer-inclusive results differ. GTX CTR reaches $0.935\times$, with a range of
$0.918$--$1.008\times$ and only one session above parity; its ECB pipeline loses in all five
sessions. No tested GTX pipeline condition has an all-five-session advantage over AES-NI.

T600 CTR provides the clearest consistent laptop result: a median pipeline speedup of
$1.354\times$ at 32~MiB, with gains in all five sessions. This corresponds to approximately
26.14\% lower paired latency. The speedup IQR is 0.030896 and
CV is 8.18\%. CTR also wins in all five sessions at 1~MiB, whereas the direction is mixed at
16~MiB. T600 ECB at 32~MiB has a median gain but loses in two sessions.

T4 CTR reaches $4.761\times$ at 32~MiB and wins in all five sessions. Its ECB pipeline reaches
only $0.893\times$ and loses in every session. Thus even a consistent resident advantage does
not establish a corresponding pipeline advantage.

\subsection{Absolute Throughput and Cipher Mode}

At 32~MiB CTR, pipeline throughput is 1188.9~MB/s on GTX, 2049.4~MB/s on T600 and 3713.9~MB/s
on T4. Resident throughput is 4585.6, 4328.9 and 9166.8~MB/s, respectively. These absolute
GPU rates are distinct from the CPU-relative speedups in Table~\ref{tab:gpu-speedups}.

T4 pipeline throughput is nearly equal for ECB and CTR at this size, approximately 3713 and
3714~MB/s. Its host AES-NI ECB throughput, however, is $5.378\times$ the CTR throughput, with
session ratios ranging from $5.250$ to $5.795\times$. The corresponding ratios on GTX and T600
are approximately $1.26\times$. The T4 disparity is consistent across sessions, but its cause
was not isolated. A large paired speedup can therefore reflect CPU mode behavior as well as
GPU throughput; it is not a direct measure of the GPU's advantage over another system.

\subsection{Size Dependence and Variability}

Throughput is not uniformly monotonic in input size. All four retained GTX mode/scope groups
show a local 16~MiB dip below both 1 and 32~MiB in all five sessions. T4 resident measurements
show that dip in four sessions, whereas its pipeline measurements show none. The size feature
therefore depends on the configuration and measurement scope.

Session ranges also matter when the median is near parity. The mixed T600 ECB outcome and
occasional GTX CTR gain at 32~MiB would be obscured by reporting only their medians. Together
with the size-dependent reversals, these observations support retaining the full workload grid
and session-level comparisons rather than selecting a single favorable measurement.
