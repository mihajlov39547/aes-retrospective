\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This study experimentally compared validated GPU-assisted AES-128 ECB and CTR encryption with
hardware-assisted CPU AES-NI across three heterogeneous configurations. It distinguished
device-resident execution from a prepared-buffer pipeline containing synchronous host--device
transfers and synchronization, using paired measurements across repeated sessions.

For 32~MiB AES-CTR buffers, the pipeline achieved median paired accelerations of $1.354\times$
on T600 and $4.761\times$ on T4 relative to their respective host implementations. Both
improvements occurred in all five sessions, including on the tested 4~GB T600 GPU.
Resident execution outperformed AES-NI at this size on every system in both modes, but the GTX
pipeline did not show a consistent advantage, and T4 ECB remained slower than its CPU reference.
Thus, a computational advantage survived data movement for selected CTR workloads, while the
outcome depended on the system, mode, and execution boundary.

These findings establish measured operating conditions for GPU-assisted bulk encryption
alongside existing CPU acceleration. They could inform workload placement in telecommunications
gateways and other services handling large data buffers, including storage and analytics
applications. Their relevance rests on matching the application's workload and data placement to
the evaluated conditions. The experiments do not establish improved IPsec ESP, AES-GCM,
packet-batching, or complete application performance. Future work should explore
an IPsec-oriented authenticated-encryption prototype evaluated across
the full packet-processing path. Measuring its throughput and latency with authentication,
protocol state, packet aggregation, and delivery included would determine whether the
demonstrated bulk AES-CTR improvement translates into an application-level benefit.
