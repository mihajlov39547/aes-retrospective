This paper presents a validated and reproducible methodological framework for experimentally comparing cryptographic primitives and their implementations in a heterogeneous CPU/GPU environment. Starting from a DES/TDEA--AES retrospective, the study does not introduce a new cryptographic algorithm, but examines how implementation paths and measurement scope affect the interpretation of performance results. Particular emphasis is placed on a paired comparison between CPU AES-NI and CUDA AES-128 implementations, with a clear separation between isolated resident/kernel measurements and practical end-to-end pipelines that include host-device data transfers. The results show that GPU resident measurements can achieve high throughput for larger inputs, while the inclusion of transfer and synchronization costs changes the conclusion in favor of the AES-NI path in the tested environment. Reduced-keyspace and avalanche-effect demonstrations are used as controlled indicators of the limits of interpreting performance and security-related metrics. The results should not be interpreted as a general upper bound on GPU AES performance; they characterize the tested baseline CUDA implementation, hardware platform, and synchronous end-to-end pipeline.
