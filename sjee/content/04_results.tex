
\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

This section presents the experimental evidence for GPU-assisted AES acceleration under
different workload sizes, cipher modes, and execution conditions. The analysis focuses on
whether GPU processing provides a measurable improvement over hardware-assisted CPU
encryption, particularly when the processing path includes data movement between the host
and the accelerator.

The results are obtained from the validated AES-128 study described in
Section~\ref{sec:methodology}. The reported performance statistics are based on five
study sessions per configuration, with paired CPU and GPU measurements. The analysis
distinguishes improvements in device-resident computation from those observed in the
prepared-buffer GPU pipeline.

The principal interest is the processing of larger encryption buffers, which provides
an experimental basis for investigating GPU-assisted bulk cryptographic workloads.
Smaller inputs are retained to establish how the observed advantage changes with
workload size.


\subsection{GPU Acceleration Across Workload Sizes}
\label{subsec:acceleration_results}

Figure~\ref{fig:aesni-scopes} presents the measured GPU acceleration across the five
evaluated input sizes, distinguishing AES-128 ECB and CTR execution and the two GPU
measurement boundaries.

  \label{fig:aesni-scopes}

The results demonstrate that the benefit of GPU acceleration depends on the amount
of work processed and the execution boundary used to evaluate the workload.

For the 32~MiB input, resident GPU execution exceeds the corresponding CPU AES-NI
reference on all three systems, in both encryption modes and in all five sessions
per configuration.

This finding establishes a consistent computational advantage for the evaluated
CUDA implementation when the input is already available in device memory.

However, resident performance represents only the encryption kernel. Its advantage
does not automatically establish faster processing when the complete buffer must
be transferred from host memory to the GPU and returned after encryption.

The prepared-buffer measurements provide a more demanding test of practical
acceleration because they include the synchronous transfer and execution sequence.

Under this broader measurement boundary, the observed results differ substantially
among systems and encryption modes. Some configurations retain a measurable advantage,
while others experience a reversal in favor of the CPU AES-NI path.

Consequently, the experiments identify GPU acceleration as a conditional processing
opportunity rather than a universally beneficial execution strategy.


\subsection{Experimentally Confirmed Bulk Encryption Improvement}
\label{subsec:bulk_acceleration}

The most relevant positive results are obtained for AES-128 CTR processing of a
32~MiB buffer.

factors for resident and prepared-buffer pipeline execution, including the observed
session ranges and the number of sessions in which the GPU pipeline is faster.

The T600 configuration provides a particularly relevant result for GPU-assisted
bulk encryption on accessible hardware.

At 32~MiB, its CUDA CTR pipeline achieves a median paired acceleration of
$1.354\times$ relative to the host AES-NI implementation. The observed range
extends from $1.129\times$ to $1.391\times$, with the GPU completing the
measured processing sequence faster in all five sessions.

The result corresponds to approximately 26.14\% lower processing latency
when expressed through the median of the paired CPU-to-GPU latency ratios.

Importantly, this improvement is not restricted to device-resident execution.
It remains observable when the defined processing interval includes
host-to-device transfer, CUDA kernel execution, device-to-host transfer,
and the synchronization required for completion.

The measured result therefore establishes that a 4~GB laptop GPU can provide
a repeatable advantage for a selected bulk AES-CTR workload even when data
movement is included in the evaluated processing sequence.

The Tesla T4 demonstrates a larger advantage under the same workload definition.

For 32~MiB CTR encryption, its prepared-buffer CUDA pipeline achieves a median
paired acceleration of $4.761\times$, with values ranging from approximately
$4.691\times$ to $5.021\times$ across the five sessions.

The GPU pipeline outperforms the selected host AES-NI implementation in every
recorded session.

These findings provide direct experimental evidence that GPU-assisted bulk
AES-CTR processing can deliver measurable improvements over a hardware-accelerated
CPU implementation under specific operating conditions.

However, the T600 and T4 speedup factors refer to different host processors.
They must not be interpreted as a direct measurement of relative GPU performance
between the two systems.

The results demonstrate the feasibility of the measured acceleration on the
corresponding complete hardware--software configurations.


\subsection{Conditions Limiting the Acceleration Benefit}
\label{subsec:acceleration_constraints}

The positive CTR results do not extend uniformly across the tested configurations.

On the GTX system, the prepared-buffer AES-128 CTR pipeline achieves a median
paired speedup of $0.935\times$ at 32~MiB.

The observed session range is $0.918$--$1.008\times$, with the GPU pipeline
outperforming AES-NI in only one of five sessions.

Although resident GPU execution is consistently faster, the broader measured
pipeline does not demonstrate a repeatable improvement over the CPU reference.

The GTX ECB pipeline also remains slower than AES-NI in all five sessions
at the same workload size.

These findings are important because they show that the computational capability
of the accelerator is not sufficient to guarantee an advantage for bulk
processing when the application requires host--device data movement.

The T4 results demonstrate a different limitation associated with cipher mode.

At 32~MiB, the T4 prepared-buffer CTR pipeline achieves a substantial
advantage, whereas the ECB pipeline reaches only $0.893\times$ relative
to the corresponding CPU AES-NI path.

The ECB pipeline is slower in every recorded session despite the resident
GPU execution remaining faster than AES-NI.

The T600 also exhibits mode-dependent behavior. Its 32~MiB ECB pipeline
has a median speedup above one, but the GPU is faster in only three
of five sessions.

Thus, a positive aggregate median does not necessarily indicate a
consistently favorable processing configuration.

The experimental evidence identifies an important practical constraint:
the conditions supporting GPU acceleration must be established for the
specific workload and encryption mode rather than inferred solely from
the presence of a parallel accelerator.

For potential bulk cryptographic applications, these results indicate
that processing volume alone is not sufficient to determine whether
GPU execution will be advantageous.


\subsection{Absolute Encryption Throughput}
\label{subsec:absolute_throughput}

Relative acceleration factors are useful for determining whether a GPU processing
path improves upon its host CPU reference. However, absolute throughput is also
necessary to characterize the processing capacity of the accelerator itself.

At 32~MiB in CTR mode, the measured prepared-buffer pipeline throughput is
approximately 1188.9~MB/s on GTX, 2049.4~MB/s on T600, and 3713.9~MB/s
on T4.

The corresponding device-resident throughput values are 4585.6~MB/s,
4328.9~MB/s, and 9166.8~MB/s, respectively.

These measurements demonstrate that the T4 provides the highest absolute
CTR throughput among the evaluated systems under this workload.

They also show that GPU pipeline throughput can differ substantially from
device-resident throughput even when the same encryption implementation
and input size are used.

The distinction matters for system design because the amount of data
that can be processed per second depends on the actual execution
sequence required to make the input available and recover the result.

A configuration with high resident throughput may not deliver a
correspondingly high host-observed processing rate.

Absolute throughput and host-relative acceleration therefore describe
different aspects of the measured processing capability.

The experiments do not isolate the individual contributions of PCIe
transfer time, synchronization, and runtime overhead sufficiently
to attribute the entire resident-to-pipeline difference to
one component.


\subsection{Cipher-Mode Dependence and Host Processing Behavior}
\label{subsec:mode_dependence}

The T4 measurements reveal an additional factor affecting the interpretation
of GPU-assisted acceleration.

For 32~MiB inputs, the baseline GPU pipeline achieves nearly identical
absolute throughput in ECB and CTR modes, approximately 3713 and
3714~MB/s, respectively.

The corresponding resident GPU rates are also similar, at approximately
9177~MB/s for ECB and 9167~MB/s for CTR.

Nevertheless, the relative acceleration factors differ markedly:
$0.893\times$ for ECB and $4.761\times$ for CTR.

The principal distinction is associated with the measured host AES-NI
execution rates.

At 32~MiB, the T4 host's AES-NI ECB throughput is approximately
$5.378\times$ its CTR throughput. The ratio ranges from
$5.250\times$ to $5.795\times$ across sessions.

By contrast, the corresponding ratios on the GTX and T600 host
configurations are approximately $1.26\times$.

The T4 host disparity is consistent across the recorded sessions,
but its underlying cause was not isolated experimentally.

Differences in cryptographic library behavior, mode-specific processing,
processor characteristics, and runtime environment may contribute,
but the available evidence does not identify a single causal mechanism.

This observation is significant because a large GPU acceleration factor
may result from the behavior of the CPU execution path as well as the
absolute performance of the GPU.

Consequently, the reported CTR improvement must be interpreted
relative to the corresponding hardware-accelerated CPU implementation.

The result does not establish that the T4 GPU itself processes CTR
several times faster than ECB.

For heterogeneous cryptographic systems, the finding reinforces
the need to assess the complete selected execution path rather
than assume that the same relative acceleration will occur
under every implementation of an encryption mode.


\subsection{Workload-Size Effects and Experimental Consistency}
\label{subsec:variability_results}

The measured performance relationships also depend on workload size.

Although larger buffers can provide more independent AES operations
for GPU execution, throughput does not increase monotonically
across all evaluated sizes and platforms.

On GTX, a local throughput reduction occurs at 16~MiB relative
to both 1~MiB and 32~MiB.

For the retained CUDA implementation, this pattern is observed
across both encryption modes and both GPU timing scopes
in all five study sessions.

On T4, resident measurements show the corresponding local
reduction in four sessions, whereas the pipeline measurements
do not exhibit the same pattern.

These differences demonstrate that the observed size dependence
is not a universal property of GPU AES execution.

The benchmark processes workload sizes in ascending order,
which also prevents complete separation of buffer-size effects
from time-dependent execution-environment behavior.

The available results therefore do not establish whether
the local reduction is caused by memory behavior, scheduling,
device clocks, or another system-level mechanism.

Session-level consistency is particularly important when the
measured acceleration approaches parity.

For example, GTX CTR at 32~MiB occasionally favors the GPU,
but the median result and directional count do not support
a consistently positive pipeline advantage.

Similarly, T600 ECB at 32~MiB has a positive median acceleration
while showing mixed results across sessions.

By contrast, the T600 and T4 32~MiB CTR pipeline improvements
remain positive in all five recorded sessions.

For the T600 CTR configuration, the paired speedup has an
interquartile range of 0.030896 and a sample coefficient
of variation of approximately 8.18\%.

These descriptive statistics provide information about
the stability of the observed advantage within the tested
sessions.

They do not constitute confidence intervals or establish
that the same performance improvement will occur on other
devices or in different execution environments.

The results therefore distinguish repeatable acceleration
under observed conditions from isolated favorable outcomes.


\subsection{Summary of Experimental Findings}
\label{subsec:results_summary}

The experiments establish three principal findings relevant
to GPU-assisted bulk AES processing.

First, device-resident CUDA execution provides a consistent
computational advantage over the evaluated AES-NI CPU path
at 32~MiB on all three systems and in both tested modes.

Second, this advantage can persist when host--device transfers
are included. The T600 and T4 systems demonstrate repeatable
prepared-buffer AES-CTR improvements at 32~MiB, confirming
that GPU-assisted bulk processing can be beneficial under
specific execution conditions.

Third, the improvement is conditional. The GTX pipeline
does not achieve a consistently positive result, and
the T4 ECB measurements demonstrate that the observed
benefit also depends on cipher mode and host implementation
behavior.

Together, these findings identify experimentally supported
opportunities and constraints for GPU-assisted bulk AES
processing.

They provide a basis for assessing heterogeneous cryptographic
processing strategies in telecommunications-oriented systems,
while the performance of actual packet batching, authenticated
encryption, and complete network processing remains outside
the scope of the present measurements.
