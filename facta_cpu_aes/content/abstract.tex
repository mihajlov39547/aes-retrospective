Efficient AES encryption is important for secure storage and communication, yet processor support alone does not determine the throughput available to software. This paper examines how library implementation and measurement scope influence CPU encryption performance. We analyze previously collected, validated AES measurements from Windows x86-64 and Oracle Cloud Arm64, with separate CBC and GCM experiments addressing key length and authenticated processing. At 32 MiB, the default OpenSSL CTR implementation reaches 4.03 GB/s on Arm64, compared with 0.21--0.22 GB/s for the evaluated alternative implementations. On Windows, its throughput advantage over PyCryptodome decreases from approximately 1.9 to 1.2 when timing expands from encryption processing to the instrumented benchmark operation. These findings show why fast cipher execution does not directly predict performance at a broader software interface. They provide a basis for selecting representative benchmarks and identifying where further optimization may benefit encryption services.
